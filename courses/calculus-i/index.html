1.1 · The Intuitive Approach

Intuitive definition of limit

If \(f(x)\) gets closer and closer to \(L\) as \(x\) gets closer and closer to \(a\) (but \(x\neq a\)), then \(\ \displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L.\) The value at \(a\) does not determine the limit — only behaviour near \(a\).
Example — a polynomial: limx→3(x²+1)

Left \(2.9,2.99,2.999\Rightarrow9.41,9.9401,9.994001\); right \(3.1,3.01,3.001\Rightarrow10.61,10.0601,10.006001\).

Both sides \(\to10\): \(\lim_{x\to3}(x^2+1)=10\). Near, not at.

Example — a hole: (x²−1)/(x−1) · one-sided: |x|/x · infinite: 1/x²

Hole: simplify to \(x+1\) for \(x\neq1\) ⇒ limit 2 — the value that fills the hole.

\(|x|/x\): right 1, left −1 ⇒ two-sided limit does not exist (jump).

\(\sin x/x\Rightarrow1\) (the most important limit, proved in §1.4). \(1/x^2\Rightarrow\infty\) — an infinite limit.

1.4 · The Squeezing Theorem

Squeezing Theorem

If \(f(x)\le g(x)\le h(x)\) near \(a\) (except possibly at \(a\)) and \(\lim f=\lim h=L\), then \(\lim g=L\). Analogy: three walkers, left–middle–right; if the outer two exit at the same door, so does the middle one.
Proof — limx→0 sin x / x = 1 (unit circle)

For \(0\lt x\lt\frac\pi2\): \(\triangle OAD=\frac{\sin x}2\), sector \(=\frac x2\), \(\triangle OAB=\frac{\tan x}2\). Nesting gives \(\sin x\le x\le\tan x\); divide and reciprocate:

\(\cos x\le\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\le1\)

Squeeze ⇒ limit 1 (the function is even, so both sides agree). \(\blacksquare\)

Examples — oscillation tamed · squeeze at infinity

\(x^2\sin\frac1x\Rightarrow0\) via \(-x^2\le\cdot\le x^2\). \(x\sin\frac1x\Rightarrow0\) via \(\pm|x|\). \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}=0\).

1.6 · Infinite Limits and Limits at Infinity

Infinite limits · asymptotes

\(\lim f=\infty\): \(\forall N\ \exists\delta:\) values exceed \(N\). \(x=a\) is a vertical asymptote if a one-sided limit is \(\pm\infty\). \(y=L\) is a horizontal asymptote if a limit at \(\pm\infty\) equals \(L\) (directions may differ).
Examples — 1/x each side · 1/x² · VAs · rational end behaviour

\(\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac1x=\infty\): \(\delta=\frac1N\); left ⇒ \(-\infty\). \(\lim_{x\to0}\frac1{x^2}=\infty\): \(\delta=\frac1{\sqrt N}\).

VAs of \(\frac{x+2}{x^2-1}\): \(x=\pm1\). \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1x=0\): \(M=\frac1\varepsilon\). Rational: \(\frac{3x^2-2x+1}{2x^2+5x-3}\to\frac32\); \(\frac{x^3-1}{x^2-4}\to\infty\).

Continuity at a point

① \(f(a)\) exists ② \(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)\) exists ③ they are equal. On \([a,b]\): continuous inside + one-sided at the endpoints.

Extreme Value Theorem

\(f\) continuous on a closed interval ⇒ absolute max and min both attained. Critical number: interior \(c\) with \(f'(c)=0\) or \(f'(c)\) undefined (Fermat). Critical numbers need not give extrema (\(x^3\) at 0).

      Common mistakes

      • A limit may exist without the function being defined at the point.
      • Check denominator zeros, root domains, and theorem hypotheses before simplifying.
      • Include the inner derivative when using the chain rule.
      • A critical number alone does not establish a maximum or minimum.
      • Include \(C\) for indefinite integrals; evaluate both endpoints for definite integrals.
      • Geometric area is nonnegative; split intervals when curve order changes.

      Glossary

      Antiderivative
      A function whose derivative equals a given function.
      Continuity
      No break at a point; limit equals value.
      Critical number
      Domain value where \(f'=0\) or \(f'\) does not exist.
      Definite integral
      A limit of sums representing net accumulation.
      Derivative
      Instantaneous rate of change; tangent slope.

      Connects differentiation and definite integration.
      Inflection point
      A point where concavity changes.
      Limit
      The value approached as the input approaches.

      A local maximum or minimum.

      A finite sum approximating accumulation or area.
